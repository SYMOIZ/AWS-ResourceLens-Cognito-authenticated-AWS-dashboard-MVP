# Running a meetup

For the person who owns an event. Owner means accountable for it happening: not doing all of this yourself, but knowing the state of every line.

Owning 2 events end to end is the core organizer requirement. The retro is also the evidence source for other people's [contribution units](../governance/membership.md#contribution-units).

## Non-negotiables

Four from the CNCF:

1. **Free to attend.** No tickets, no fees, no "contribution towards food".
2. **Vendor neutral content.** No product pitches. See [speaking.md](speaking.md).
3. **Registration on the [group page](https://ocgroups.dev/cncf/group/cn-khi).** Not a Google Form, not WhatsApp replies. That page is the CNCF's record of our attendance.
4. **An event at least every 90 days**, or the group risks being marked inactive.

One of ours: **record actual attendance at the door**, not RSVPs. Membership units, the CNCF's 10-attendee threshold, and whether the next venue takes us seriously all depend on a real number.

## Timeline

Six weeks is comfortable, four is possible. Under three the room will be half empty however good the talks are.

**T-6 weeks.** Lock date and venue. Open the event on the group page even with speakers to be announced; the registration count is what you show venues and sponsors. Open a planning issue labelled `event`, name yourself owner, paste this checklist in.

**T-5 weeks.** Confirm speakers. Two talks of 25 to 30 minutes plus one 10-minute lightning talk fits a 3-hour slot and leaves time for people to talk to each other. Get titles, abstracts, bios and headshots now; chasing them at T-1 is how announcements slip.

**T-4 weeks.** Announce everywhere the same day: group page, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, `#cloud-native-karachi`, both WhatsApp groups. Ask Members to reshare, which is what [baseline requirement 6](../governance/membership.md#baseline-requirements) asks of them. Confirm food and any sponsor.

**T-3 to T-1 weeks.** One reminder a week, each with something new: a speaker profile, venue and parking. Confirm the AV chain specifically: projector, the cable that fits the speaker's laptop, a microphone that works. Cap attendance at real room capacity.

**T-2 days.** Reconfirm every speaker by voice or direct reply, not by hoping. Have a backup lightning talk. Prepare the check-in list. Final reminder with address, map link and start time.

**Event day.** Arrive 60 minutes early, test AV with an actual laptop, open check-in 30 minutes before the start. Assume a late start and order the running so the first talk is not the one that suffers. Record attendance, photograph the room wide, record talks if you can. Thank the venue and sponsor from the front. Leave the venue better than you found it.

**T+3 days.** Open the retro, labelled `event` so it stays findable when someone cites it for a contribution unit, publish slides and recordings, thank speakers and volunteers by name, and open the pull request incrementing `events_held` in [members.yaml](../members/members.yaml).

## The retro issue

- Registrations, actual attendance, no-show rate.
- Every volunteer named, with what they did. People cite this for contribution units, so leaving someone out costs them something real.
- Speakers, with slides and recordings.
- Venue and sponsor, and whether to use them again.
- What broke: AV, timing, food, check-in queue.
- Cost, if money moved.

Write it even when the event went well. That is where the repeatable parts are.

## Karachi specifics

- **Saturday, roughly 2pm to 5pm.** The weekend here is Saturday and Sunday, and this avoids the Sunday family default and weekday traffic. Weekday evenings mean nobody arrives before 8pm.
- **Traffic decides your venue, not its quality.** Pick something on a main corridor and say in the announcement how long it takes from the other side of the city. Nobody crosses Karachi at 6pm on a weekday for a meetup.
- **Ramadan and monsoon shape the calendar.** Schedule around Ramadan or run post-Iftar with a later, smaller crowd. July and August risk being washed out.
- **Expect 40 to 60 percent no-shows** and cap accordingly. Free events are cheap to abandon everywhere. A reminder 24 hours out is the cheapest fix.
- **Venue before anything else.** University labs, company offices and coworking spaces are the realistic options. A thank-you from the front plus a logo on the announcement is usually enough, and it buys no talk.

## Sponsorship

A sponsor can cover food, venue or swag. They get a thank-you from the front, a logo on the announcement, and a mention in the retro. They never get a speaking slot, the attendee list, or influence over which talks are accepted.

Say this in writing before anything is agreed. A sponsor who walks away on hearing it has saved you a worse conversation on the day.

Money moving needs a majority of organizers and goes in the retro. The group holds no bank account; sponsors pay the caterer or venue directly.
